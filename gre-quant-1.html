GRE · Quantitative Reasoning · Lesson 1

Fractions Review

Fractions show up everywhere on the GRE: in ratios, percents, probability, and word problems. In this lesson you will make predictions, watch a short review video, and then show what you learned with 12 GRE-style questions.

  • 1 · Before you watch
  • 2 · Watch & take notes
  • 3 · Show what you learned
  • 4 · Analyze your errors

Step 1

Before You Watch: Make Predictions

Answer quickly, without a calculator. Don't worry about being right: the goal is to find out what you already believe. You will check these after the video.

Step 2

Watch the Review Video

Guided notes: listen for these

Pause the video when you hear the answer. Write in your own words (English or your first language).

  1. Why can you multiply fractions straight across, but you need a common denominator to add them?
  2. What does it mean to "divide by a fraction"? Why does dividing by 12 make a number larger?
  3. How do you change a mixed number like 213 into an improper fraction, and why is that useful?

Your notes are not saved when you leave the page. Copy them into your study notebook.

Key ideas at a glance

Simplify: divide top and bottom by the same factor. 1824 = 34

Add / subtract: common denominator first. 12 + 13 = 36 + 26 = 56

Multiply: top × top, bottom × bottom. Cancel first to save time.

Divide: multiply by the reciprocal. 23 ÷ 45 = 23 × 54 = 56

Compare: cross-multiply. ab vs cd → compare a·d with b·c.

"Of" means multiply: 23 of 30 = 20.

Step 3

Show What You Learned: 12 GRE-Style Questions

These use the four GRE question types: Quantitative Comparison, Multiple Choice (one answer), Multiple Choice (select all that apply), and Numeric Entry. A real GRE Quant section has 12 questions in 21 minutes, so aim for about 1¾ minutes per question. Work on paper first; use a calculator only to check.

Step 4

Analyze Your Errors

Strong test-takers don't just count wrong answers. They ask why they missed each one. For every question you missed, choose the main reason. The table fills in as you check answers.

Check some answers in Step 3 first. Any question you miss will appear here.

Practice with AI

Copy this prompt into ChatGPT or Claude:

"I am preparing for the GRE. Give me 5 Quantitative Comparison questions about fractions, one at a time. Wait for my answer before explaining. Focus on comparing fractions and on fractions with variables."

Then check the AI's math yourself. AI sometimes makes arithmetic mistakes, and finding them is good practice.
