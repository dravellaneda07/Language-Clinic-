Questions 1–5: Text Completion

For each question, select one entry for each blank from the corresponding column of choices. Fill all blanks in the way that best completes the text.
1Even the charisma and technical prowess of two fine actors are not ______ the task of fully invigorating a gray domestic drama with a tired tale to tell.
Invigorating a “gray” drama with a “tired” story is presented as a challenge. “Even” followed by the actors’ strengths signals that those strengths still fell short. Only adequate for expresses that the actors were not up to the task.
2Even as the economy struggled, the secretary stood by his ______ long-term outlook, saying that technology was allowing businesses to make deep-rooted improvements in their productivity, the best indicator of an economy’s ability to grow.
“Even as the economy struggled” sets up a contrast: the secretary believes technology will drive growth, so his outlook is optimistic. Sanguine means optimistic. Equivocal, ambivalent, and irresolute all suggest wavering, but he “stood by” his view. Arcane (obscure) does not fit a rationale he explains plainly.
3The incipient (i) ______ regarding taxes could affect trade between the two countries much more than the (ii) ______ banana imports, which has been going on for years. Unfortunately, the trade regulators seem to be ignoring both disagreements.
Blank (i)
Blank (ii)
The key is at the end: “both disagreements.” Each blank must name a disagreement. Row (pronounced to rhyme with “cow”) means a quarrel, and festering dispute over describes a long-running conflict, which matches “going on for years.” The other choices do not describe disagreements.
4The governor has considerable political talents, but as a speaker he is far less (i) ______ than his opponent, whose oratorical skills are (ii) ______.
Blank (i)
Blank (ii)
“But” contrasts his strong political talents with weaker speaking ability. “Far less ______” must be negative overall, so the blank itself must be positive: adroit (skillful). Unconvincing and prolix (wordy) are negative and would create a double negative. The opponent is therefore a far better speaker, so Blank (ii) must be strongly positive: breathtaking.
5Kept (i) ______ by cloying commercial radio and clueless record executives, the American popular music scene has frequently depended on cities at the edges of the cultural map to provide a much-needed shot of (ii) ______. The momentary (iii) ______ what the next big thing is seems to come out of nowhere—as if someone blows a whistle only those in the know can hear, and suddenly record executives and journalists are crawling all over what had previously been an obscure locale.
Blank (i)
Blank (ii)
Blank (iii)
Start with Blank (iii). The whistle image and executives and journalists “crawling all over” one place show many people suddenly agreeing on the same thing: a consensus about the next big thing. That next big thing is something new, so the outlying cities supply originality (Blank ii). Needing a shot of originality means the scene had been stuck in old habits, made hidebound (narrow and inflexible) by bland radio and clueless executives (Blank i).

Passage for Question 6

Despite a dramatic increase in the number of people riding bicycles for recreation in Parkville, a recent report by the Parkville Department of Transportation shows that the number of accidents involving bicycles has decreased for the third consecutive year.

6Which of the following, if true during the last three years, best reconciles the apparent discrepancy in the facts?
We need a reason accidents could fall while ridership rises. Stricter enforcement and required safety training would reduce accidents per rider, so D resolves the puzzle. A and C would put even more bikes on the road, which deepens the puzzle. B concerns automobile accidents, not bicycle accidents. E removes a safety measure, which would, if anything, increase accidents.

Questions 7–10: Reading Comprehension

For Questions 7–9, consider each choice separately and select all that apply. For Question 10, select one answer choice.
Passage for Questions 7–9

What makes a worker ant perform one particular task rather than another? From the 1970s to the mid-1980s, researchers emphasized internal factors within individual ants, such as polymorphism, the presence in the nest of workers of different shapes and sizes, each suited to a particular task. Other elements then considered to have primary influence upon an ant’s career were its age—it might change tasks as it got older—and its genetics. However, subsequent ant researchers have focused on external prompts for behavior. In advocating this approach, Deborah Gordon cites experiments in which intervention in a colony’s makeup perturbed worker activity. By removing workers or otherwise altering the nest conditions, researchers were able to change the tasks performed by individual workers.

7According to the passage, which of the following factors were considered from the 1970s to the mid-1980s to influence the division of labor among a colony’s worker ants?

Select all that apply.

The passage says researchers in that period considered an ant’s age and its genetics (inherited traits) to be primary influences, so A and B are correct. The passage never mentions experiences outside the nest, so C is incorrect.
8It can be inferred from the passage that Gordon and earlier researchers would agree with which of the following statements about worker ants?

Select all that apply.

Look for common ground. Earlier researchers thought an ant “might change tasks as it got older,” and Gordon cites experiments in which workers’ tasks changed. Both groups accept that tasks can change, so C is correct. A is Gordon’s external-factor view; nothing suggests earlier researchers held it. B may fit some earlier researchers, but nothing suggests Gordon would agree.
9The last sentence has which of the following functions in the passage?

Select all that apply.

The last sentence shows that changing external conditions changed ants’ tasks, which is evidence for Gordon’s external-factor view. So C is correct. A fails because the passage never describes any experiments by earlier researchers. B fails because the sentence describes what the experiments did but does not argue that the method was sound.
Passage for Question 10

James W. Coleman’s book on John Edgar Wideman’s literary career addresses the needs of a general, if well-read, public rather than the esoteric vanities of scholarly specialists, whom he neither ignores nor flatters. To assume the former audience was familiar with every work Wideman ever penned would have been pretentious. Instead, Coleman furnishes more than ample descriptive criticism and background information, avoiding the cryptic allusiveness that is favored by some academic critics but that discourages the undergraduate audience he likely envisioned. Unfortunately, this accent on bringing serious Wideman criticism to a broader audience often frustrates the reader who wishes that announced themes, techniques, and stylistic devices would not whisk by as quickly as world capitals on a seven-day package tour of the globe.

A tour that “whisks” through world capitals in seven days touches each one only briefly. The comparison criticizes Coleman for moving too fast through important ideas, so the treatment is cursory (hasty and superficial). Inclusive and broad based describe range, not the problem of speed. Questions 11–14: Sentence Equivalence

Select the two answer choices that, when used to complete the sentence, fit the meaning of the sentence as a whole and produce completed sentences that are alike in meaning.
11At first glance, Watkins Park, with its meandering stream and its thicket of greenery, seems ______; however, upon closer inspection, one is quickly reminded that the park is in the middle of a major city.
“However” contrasts the park’s first impression with its city location, so the blank must mean countrylike. Bucolic and pastoral both mean rural and peaceful. Remarkable and noteworthy are a synonym pair, but they do not create the contrast with “middle of a major city.” Urban says the opposite of what is needed.
12A particular bacterium that has never encountered a particular virus will usually succumb to it, a ______ that may, surprisingly, be beneficial to the colony in which the bacterium lives.
The blank names the bacterium’s tendency to be killed by an unfamiliar virus. Susceptibility and characteristic are not synonyms in general, but both can describe this trait, so they produce sentences alike in meaning. Theory and hypothesis form a tempting pair, but the sentence describes how bacteria behave, not an idea about them.
13It is a testimony to Roth’s ______ that he could not quite bring himself to write a book as dull and flat as his original conception for his novel Everyman seemed to demand.
The irony is that Roth was too gifted to produce the dull, flat book his plan called for. Talent and genius express that idea. Deterioration and decline are a synonym pair but would not contrast with a “dull and flat” book. Persistence and ambition each fit loosely, but they do not mean the same thing.
14The mayor is more ideologically consistent than is widely believed: her long-term commitment to tax reform, for example, is not indicative of ______.
People wrongly think she is inconsistent; her long-term commitment shows she is not. The blank must name the opposite of consistency: capriciousness and fickleness. Callousness/insensitivity and perspicacity/clearheadedness are also synonym pairs, but neither contrasts with consistency.

Question 15: Critical Reasoning

Select one answer choice.
Passage for Question 15

Geographers and historians have traditionally held the view that Antarctica was first sighted around 1820, but some sixteenth-century European maps show a body that resembles the polar landmass, even though explorers of the period never saw it. Some scholars, therefore, argue that the continent must have been discovered and mapped by the ancients, whose maps are known to have served as models for the European cartographers.

15Which of the following, if true, is most damaging to the inference drawn by the scholars?
The scholars assume that a southern landmass on ancient maps means the ancients actually saw it. E offers another explanation: the ancients may have drawn it because their theory said a landmass should be there, not because anyone discovered it. A concerns modern discovery, which is irrelevant. B describes a period that likely came before the ancient mapmakers, and a smaller landmass would still exist. C does not matter, because the argument does not depend on how many maps there are. D is a general claim about such theories and does not address this argument.
