GRE Preparation

A free study space for the GRE General Test. Learn the format, follow a study plan, build academic vocabulary, review math one topic at a time, practice, and learn how to write the essay.

The GRE changes over time. Always confirm dates, fees, and rules at ets.org/gre, and check whether your program requires the GRE at all.

Part 1

Know the Test

The GRE General Test (current format, in effect since September 2023) takes about 1 hour 58 minutes and has five sections:

SectionQuestionsTime
Analytical Writing (always first)1 essay: Analyze an Issue30 min
Verbal Reasoning 11218 min
Verbal Reasoning 21523 min
Quantitative Reasoning 11221 min
Quantitative Reasoning 21526 min

Verbal and Quantitative can appear in either order. The test adapts by section: your performance on the first Verbal (or Quant) section determines how difficult the second one is. You can skip, mark, and return to questions within a section. An on-screen calculator is provided for Quant.

Scores

Verbal and Quantitative: 130–170 each (1-point steps). Analytical Writing: 0–6 (half-point steps).

Source

Structure confirmed on the official ETS test structure page.

Part 2

Build a Study Plan

Most learners need 6–12 weeks. Here is a sample 8-week plan at 6–8 hours per week. Adjust it after your first diagnostic test.

Week 1

Take a full official practice test as a baseline. Record your errors by type, not just your score.

Weeks 2–3

Verbal foundations: 15 new vocabulary words per week, Text Completion and Sentence Equivalence drills. Quant review: arithmetic, algebra, ratios, percents.

Weeks 4–5

Reading Comprehension with timed passages. Quant: geometry, data interpretation, quantitative comparison strategy. Write one Issue essay per week.

Weeks 6–7

Mixed timed sections. Review every mistake: why did you miss it (content, misread, timing, careless)?

Week 8

Second full practice test under real timing. Light review only in the last two days. Rest before test day.

The real test gives you an on-screen calculator, but most fraction and number questions are faster on paper. Use the calculator to check, not to think.

Part 5

Analytical Writing: Analyze an Issue

You get a short statement on a general topic and 30 minutes to write a focused, well-supported essay taking a position.

A simple structure

1. Introduction: restate the issue, state your position (and its limits). 2. Body 1: strongest reason + a specific example. 3. Body 2: second reason + a specific example. 4. Body 3: consider the opposing view and respond to it. 5. Conclusion: return to your position; do not just repeat it.

Plan for 3–5 minutes, write for about 22, and keep 3 minutes to proofread. Clarity, development, and organization matter more than fancy vocabulary.
